# A-Level Maths Revision Checklist (free, printable)

A free **A-Level Maths revision checklist**, topic by topic, covering every section of the Pure, Statistics and Mechanics content that all exam boards share (AQA, Edexcel, OCR and OCR MEI). Rate each topic red, amber or green as you revise, see what's left, print it or save it as a PDF. No sign-up, no app, no tracking.

Built by [Korra Academy](https://korra.academy/) — the team behind **[Korra.Study](https://korra.study/)**, live online exam preparation and study-skills tutoring.

## How to use it

1. Download [`checklist.html`](checklist.html) (or clone this repo) and double-click it. It opens in your browser and works offline.
2. Click each topic to cycle it through **red** (can't do it), **amber** (can do it with notes or hints) and **green** (correct, unaided, timed, twice on different days).
3. Your ratings save in the browser automatically, so you can come back to it all year.
4. Hit **Print** for a paper copy with the current colours, or **Reset** to start again.

The topic list below is the same one the app uses. Copy it into your notes if you'd rather tick on paper.

## Year 1 / AS content

### Pure Mathematics
- [ ] **Proof** — proof by deduction, proof by exhaustion, disproof by counter-example
- [ ] **Indices and surds** — laws of indices, rationalising denominators
- [ ] **Quadratics** — completing the square, the discriminant, quadratic graphs, quadratic inequalities
- [ ] **Simultaneous equations** — linear/linear and linear/quadratic, graphical interpretation
- [ ] **Inequalities** — linear and quadratic, set notation, regions on graphs
- [ ] **Polynomials** — expanding, factorising, factor theorem, algebraic division
- [ ] **Graphs and transformations** — sketching cubics, quartics and reciprocals; translations and stretches
- [ ] **Straight lines** — gradient, parallel and perpendicular lines, equation forms
- [ ] **Circles** — equation of a circle, tangents, chords, perpendicular bisectors
- [ ] **Binomial expansion** — positive integer powers, nCr notation
- [ ] **Trigonometry** — sine and cosine rules, area of a triangle, graphs of sin, cos and tan
- [ ] **Trig identities and equations** — tan = sin/cos, sin² + cos² = 1, solving in a given interval
- [ ] **Exponentials and logarithms** — e^x, laws of logs, solving a^x = b, growth and decay, log graphs for y = ax^n and y = kb^x
- [ ] **Differentiation** — from first principles, powers of x, tangents and normals, increasing/decreasing, stationary points
- [ ] **Integration** — reverse of differentiation, definite integrals, area under a curve
- [ ] **Vectors in 2D** — magnitude and direction, position vectors, vector geometry

### Statistics
- [ ] **Sampling** — population and sample; random, systematic, stratified, quota and opportunity sampling
- [ ] **Data presentation** — histograms, box plots, cumulative frequency, scatter diagrams, outliers and cleaning data
- [ ] **Location and spread** — mean, median, quartiles, variance, standard deviation, coding
- [ ] **Probability** — Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, mutually exclusive and independent events
- [ ] **Discrete distributions** — discrete uniform and binomial distributions, and when the binomial applies
- [ ] **Hypothesis testing (binomial)** — null and alternative hypotheses, significance level, one- and two-tailed tests, critical regions
- [ ] **Large data set** — the structure of your board's data set and the typical questions on it

### Mechanics
- [ ] **Quantities and units** — SI units, modelling assumptions (particle, light, smooth, inextensible)
- [ ] **Constant acceleration** — suvat equations, vertical motion under gravity
- [ ] **Motion graphs** — displacement-time and velocity-time graphs, gradient and area
- [ ] **Variable acceleration** — differentiating and integrating with respect to time
- [ ] **Forces and Newton's laws** — F = ma, weight, normal reaction, tension, connected particles, pulleys

## Year 2 / A2 content

### Pure Mathematics
- [ ] **Proof by contradiction** — including the irrationality of √2 and infinitely many primes
- [ ] **Algebraic and partial fractions** — simplifying, splitting into partial fractions
- [ ] **Functions** — domain and range, composite and inverse functions, the modulus function
- [ ] **Further transformations** — combined transformations, modulus graphs
- [ ] **Parametric equations** — converting to Cartesian form, differentiating parametrically
- [ ] **Binomial expansion (any rational n)** — validity, approximations, use with partial fractions
- [ ] **Sequences and series** — arithmetic and geometric, sum to infinity, sigma notation, recurrence relations
- [ ] **Radians** — arc length, sector area, small-angle approximations
- [ ] **Reciprocal and inverse trig** — sec, cosec, cot, arcsin, arccos, arctan and their graphs
- [ ] **Trig identities (Year 2)** — 1 + tan² = sec², addition formulae, double-angle formulae, R cos(x − α) form
- [ ] **Differentiation (Year 2)** — chain, product and quotient rules; trig, exponential and log functions; implicit differentiation; connected rates of change
- [ ] **Integration (Year 2)** — standard functions, substitution, by parts, partial fractions, trig identities, area between curves
- [ ] **Differential equations** — separating variables, modelling growth and decay
- [ ] **Numerical methods** — change of sign, iteration x = g(x), Newton–Raphson, trapezium rule
- [ ] **Vectors in 3D** — magnitude, distance between points, geometric problems

### Statistics
- [ ] **Regression and correlation** — interpreting regression lines, product moment correlation coefficient, hypothesis test for correlation
- [ ] **Conditional probability** — P(A|B), two-way tables, modelling assumptions
- [ ] **Normal distribution** — standardising, using a calculator or tables, finding μ and σ, normal approximation to the binomial
- [ ] **Hypothesis testing (normal)** — testing a sample mean

### Mechanics
- [ ] **Moments** — turning effect, equilibrium of rigid bodies, uniform rods
- [ ] **Forces at angles** — resolving, inclined planes, friction and the coefficient of friction
- [ ] **Projectiles** — horizontal and vertical components, time of flight, range, maximum height
- [ ] **Kinematics in 2D** — suvat with vectors, variable acceleration using vectors
- [ ] **Applications of Newton's laws** — connected particles on slopes, pulleys with friction

## How to revise A-Level Maths with this list

- **Red means "do a worked example first".** Watch or read one, then cover it and redo it. Don't attempt exam questions on a red topic; that is how people decide they "can't do maths".
- **Amber is where the marks are.** Most students spend too long on greens because they feel good. A session on an amber topic moves your grade; a session on a green topic only protects it.
- **Green needs two dates.** Right once is luck. Right twice, unaided, on different days, is a green. Write the dates on the sheet.
- **Past papers from the first week.** Pick questions by topic from past papers rather than working through whole papers until the last six weeks. Your board's website has every past paper and mark scheme free.
- **Mark against the mark scheme, not your feeling.** Method marks matter: write the line that earns the mark even when the final answer is wrong.
- **Mechanics and Statistics are a third of the marks.** Students who "leave applied until later" rarely get to it. Rotate: two Pure sessions, then one applied.

## FAQ

**What topics are in A-Level Maths?**
Every board follows the same Department for Education content: Pure Mathematics (proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences and series, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation, integration, numerical methods, vectors), Statistics (sampling, data presentation, probability, statistical distributions, hypothesis testing) and Mechanics (quantities and units, kinematics, forces and Newton's laws, moments). The checklist above lists them topic by topic, split into Year 1 and Year 2.

**Is the A-Level Maths content the same for AQA, Edexcel and OCR?**
Yes. Since the 2017 reform the content has been set nationally, so the topics are identical across boards. What differs is the paper structure, the question style, the large data set used in Statistics and the formula booklet. Revise the topics from any source; practise the papers from your own board.

**How is A-Level Maths assessed?**
Three written papers at the end of Year 2, each two hours long, with a calculator allowed in all of them. Roughly two-thirds of the marks are Pure Mathematics and one-third are Statistics and Mechanics combined. The exact split between papers differs by board, so check your own specification.

**How long should I spend revising each topic?**
Until it is green by the definition above, not for a fixed number of hours. A typical pattern is one 25-minute session to move a topic from red to amber, then three or four spaced sessions of exam questions to move it from amber to green. Use a revision timetable to space those sessions; our free [revision timetable generator](https://github.com/korra-academy/gcse-revision-timetable) does the spacing automatically and works for A-Level.

**When should I start revising for A-Level Maths?**
Keep revising Year 1 content throughout Year 2, because it is examined again in the final papers. Start structured, checklist-driven revision by January of Year 2 at the latest, and move to mostly timed past-paper questions from Easter.

**Does this cover Further Maths?**
No. Further Maths has its own content (complex numbers, matrices, further calculus and optional applied modules) and needs a separate list. This checklist is for the single A-Level Mathematics qualification.

**Is my data stored anywhere?**
No. The app runs entirely in your browser and saves your ratings in local storage on your own device. Nothing is uploaded.
